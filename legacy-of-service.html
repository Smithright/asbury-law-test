The President's EscortThe President's Escort

My father, Lloyd Thomas Asbury, graduated from West Point in 1963 as the number-one military graduate of his class. His First Class (senior) year he was the “King of Beast” — the cadet who ran Beast Barracks — a role remembered in The Long Gray Line, which describes the King of Beast without ever naming him. Chosen by his classmates for the honor, he escorted President John F. Kennedy onto the field at the Army–Navy Game, the entire Corps of Cadets at attention behind them.

My father told me that President Kennedy, taken with this photograph, promised to sign it for him. He was assassinated before he ever had the chance — and so this historic photograph remains, to this day, unsigned.

He later returned to West Point as a professor of French — which is where I was born and spent the first three years of my life — and served as an instructor at the Army's Airborne and Ranger school, training the Rangers who came after him.

Among the Corps' FinestAmong the Corps' Finest

Ranked first in military aptitude and selected among the cadet leaders of his class, Lloyd stood at the front of the Long Gray Line. Neither of his parents had schooling beyond the third grade — yet they founded the West Point Parents' Club of Michigan, and he prized education his whole life, earned a master's degree in French in Paris, and taught at the Academy that made him.
